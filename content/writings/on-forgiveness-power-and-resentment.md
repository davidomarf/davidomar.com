title: "On Forgiveness, Power, and Resentment"

---

I'm not someone who would consider himself resentful.

I'm very easy to forgive. And I'm very prone to empathize with anyone who's
acted on my detriment. Of course it hurts. Of course I consider pulling myself
of the interaction. Of course I evaluate the quality of the relationship with
the person. But I don't feel resentment. And I understand that there are many
reasons that could've caused the person to act in such way.

I'm very easy to forgive someone.

And I also recently learned that I'm pretty bad at holding power within an
interaction. Power is uncomfortable for me.

And when there's a damaged relationship that requires forgiveness and repair,
it's very easy for a power shift to surge. If any decides to leave, of course
there's no need to maintain the relationship. But if both people decide to stay,
and both people want to work on the repairment, it usually results in a person
who evaluates the progress, and one that works to make it happen.

This evaluation gives the hurt person power. But power has no place in love.

I'm recently reading All About Love, by bell hooks.

> Bell hooks wrote in All About Love that power struggles are fundamentally
> incompatible with love, which requires a willingness to surrender attachment
> to personal power for true connection and vulnerability. She argued that
> societies built on domination and fear cultivate lovelessness and separation,
> whereas love is an active choice to connect with others, especially those
> different from ourselves. For love to flourish, one must be willing to move
> beyond the societal emphasis on control and power, embracing instead a love
> ethic that fosters vulnerability, trust, and mutual accountability.

---

And holding this power, the power to forgive, and the power to accept repair is
a very uncomfortable position for me.

I realise it just now, but I've never been someone who enjoys position of power.
Not even when it's expected, but even less so when there's the expectation that
there shouldn't be power differences.

I've tried to build all my relationships without power disparity. At least
consciously. Even in things when there are evident differences, like income,
it's been shared in a way that doesn't create a power imbalance.

However with forgiveness you cannot do that. The only way to level the field
would be to also ask for forgiveness for something that feels equally bad for
the other person the way that the original hurt feels bad for you.

But that'd be acting with malice.
