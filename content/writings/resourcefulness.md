---

Resourcefulness is valuable. 
It allows you to operate, function, build, and function without having to have
an environment that's highly thriving. 
It's exactly what allows you to thrive in an unthriving environment.

It's meaningful. And it's something I've recently learned I possess. 

In relationships, at work, at life.

But also, when in excess, can turn into something that specifically obstaculizes
work on the environment itself.

----

From an engineering perspective: I can work with our current APIs. I can create
workarounds and make the data work, architecture and everything work towards
what's needed. 

But there's a point in which the responsibility is not in: making a working
solution out of what we have. But: reworking what we have so the solution comes
more naturally.

And just to be clear, I'm not suggesting that it's one thing or the other.
Everything, and this includes "resoursefulness", is a spectrum. 

The thing is talking with everyone involved so that it's clearer when we should
use our resourcefulness and make the best out of what we already have, and when
we should voice out concerns about the current state being unideal and something
that, even if it's possible to make work, should be redone.

----

From a perspective on relationships, the way I see it is that resourcefulness is
what allows you to find love gestures from the people you're related to.
Specifically to romantic relationships: resourcefulness alows you to interpret
acts as loveful acts, which allow you to keep yourself feeling loved, which
allows you to think that is still a good idea to remain inside a relationship.

I guess you see where this becomes dangerous. There's "too much
resourcefulness". And it's not a healthy place for you. When you start to feel
that an unhealthy environment is good because you can make it work, you're lost,
and you're in urgent need for the necessary self-love that allows you to walk
out of a relationship that does not expect you to be resourcful to feel happy.
