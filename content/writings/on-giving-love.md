I love Fleabag.

I've watched it a few times now. But more than the entire show, I have scenes
that I love rewatching every once in a while. And I'm not necessarily talking
about my fascination with the Hot Priest.

I have many: "That's the very reason why they put rubbers on the end of pencils".
"Get your hands off my miscarriage". "It looks like a pencil. -- It looks chic".

But one that I've thought about multiple times when trying to influence my life,
is:

> -- I don't know what to do with it
> -- With what?
> -- With all the love I have for her. I don't know where to put it now.
> -- I'll take it. No, I'm serious, it sounds lovely. I'll have it. You have to
> give it to me. It's got to go somewhere.

It's got to go somewhere.

Bell Hooks writes about building masculinities that are beneficial to society
and fight against patriarchy.

And one of the things she mentions as useful is: teaching men about love.
Encouraging and celebrating communal love.

When men learn to affirm themselves and others, giving this soul care, then they
area on the path to wholeness. When men are able to do little acts of rmercy,
they can be in communion with others without the need to dominate. No longer
separate, no longer apart, they bring a wholeness that can be joined with the
wholeness of others. This is interbeing. As whole people they can experience
joy. Unlike happiness, joy is a lasting state than can be sustained even when
everything is not the way we want it to be. In the essay "Celebrating Life"
Jesuit priest Henri Nouwen declares that "where there is joy there is life". 

Men of integrity are not ashamed to serve. They are caretakes, guardians,
keepers of the flame. They know joy.

Nathaniel Brandon writes, on The Six Pillars of Self Esteem:

> I am responsible for accepting or choosing the values by which I live. If I live

> imagine that they are just "my nature", just "who I am", and to avoid
> recognizing that choice is involved. If I am willing to recognize that choices
> and decisions are crucial when values are adopted, then I can take a fresh look
