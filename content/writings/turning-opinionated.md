I'm finally experiencing the act of having strong opinions on topics.
Even if the strong opinion is about (equilibrium | balance | not having strong
opinions).

It's hard reaching agreement. And it's hard to build tools that serves everyone
in the way they expect.

For example in Netflix some our internal tooling has many users. Which by the
way are also very power-users and know how to work and work around things. 

And some toolings might come short for them. Also what if they try to work
around the tool limitations and constraints and they get exposed to the
complexity behind the abstraction.

----

I think once you start being aware that your work has impact, that your
contributions are valuable and whatnot, you might start to turn opinionated. 

Working in a certain way has worked for you in the past. Using tool A instead of
tool B has worked well in your past projects. You get an opinion from them and
you try to defend it since if more people followed, they'll just realize how
good it is to work under those terms. But most importantly, it'll be an
environment in which you will be performing at your best. 

----

So is turning opinionated a step in achieving an environment that allows you to
perform at your best? 

---

Is there a bias in which you consider your opinions are not opinions but facts,
hence you consider you're not opinionated? 

I'm someone who historically has adapted to what the environment is. I might not
feel that it allows me to do my best, but also I think it's working for others..



And fucking hell if things are complex.

The grass is always greener on the other side. What's stopping you from having
the environment that allows you to perform the best you can? Sometimes those
limitations are the trade-offs you're paying for some other pros. 

And it's very easy to focus only on what's missing. On what you lack. All while
you got used to the pros that those trade-offs came as the cost of.

You can mitigate the cons, but you can only go so far before you undo the pros.
And now you'll miss that, and in comparison that'll weigh you down more than the
achieved improvement.

We have this bias in which risk is only worth taking if the expected payout is
x times higher than the expected cost. Because in our minds, a small inconnienve
is as big as a big improvement.


There's this song from Los Prisioneros, called Nunca Quedas Mal Con Nadie. While
ths song is more specific about the "target", being artists, in general it is a critique of people who try to please everyone and avoid taking a stand
on important issues. It addresses themes of conformity, superficiality, and the
tendency to avoid conflict by not expressing genuine opinions or beliefs. The
lyrics highlight the idea that trying to be agreeable to everyone often leads to
a lack of authenticity and integrity.

When you adapt to an environment that someone else is shaping, you're just
accomodating everyone else's needs instead of yours. You don't voice your needs,
your opinions, and you just adapt to what other people is already using. 

Now yes, that might be an environment they're used to and can more easily
thrive. But it can also be that they haven't been exposed to the context that
made you reach your current opinions. So voice them out, share them, and
proactively build little sandboxes so people get to experience what, in your
opinion, is an improvement.
